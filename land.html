The Land

A wooded hillside tract of roughly seven acres in Miami Township, Hamilton County, Ohio, in the hill country above the Ohio River.

Geology

The tract sits in the Kope Formation, layers of interbedded shale and limestone that make up much of the Cincinnati region's hill country. These hillsides are steep and thin-soiled, and the vegetation covering them does real work holding the ground in place.

Hydrology and terrain

The ground falls away steeply from the ridge to a low point where two creeks meet. That confluence, and the riparian corridor along it, is the low end of the tract and the reason the land holds water and moves it the way it does.

Forest

A mature canopy covers the slope. On ground this steep, that canopy is doing real work — holding soil, slowing water, keeping the hillside intact. The tract is part of the broader belt of Ohio River hillside forest that regional hillside-protection groups have spent years working to keep forested.

Rare and notable plants

The tract holds mature specimens of Torreya taxifolia, the Florida torreya — a federally endangered conifer, growing here well north of its native Gulf Coast range, as part of the wider assisted-migration effort underway for the species.

It also holds American chestnut (Castanea dentata), the tree that once dominated eastern forests before chestnut blight reduced it to stump sprouts across nearly all of its range, and buffalo clover, a native legume of this part of the Ohio River Valley.

Locations, counts, and directions for any of these plantings aren't published here; their presence on the tract is a matter of stewardship, not a destination.
